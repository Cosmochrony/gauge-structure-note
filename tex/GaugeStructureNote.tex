  \begin{abstract}
    The gauge structure sub-programme identifies the internal symmetry groups that survive
    as invariants of admissible non-injective projection.
    Its central question is: which internal symmetry groups are forced by the admissibility
    structure of $\Pi$, rather than postulated?
    The sub-programme establishes the following chain:
    \[
      \Pi^{-1}(O_n)
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \text{phase fibre}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \UU(1)
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \ImH \simeq \su(2)
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \SU(2)
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \text{colour triplet}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \SU(3).
    \]
    The status of each factor is distinct:
    $\UU(1)$ is structural (Hopf fibre of $\Pi \simeq S^3$);
    $\SU(2)$ is closed analytically (O27--O30, Q6a);
    $\SU(3)$ is established unconditionally on the colour-adapted graph and on the standard
    graph at all four analytical levels (rank, averaged, effective-exponent, and pointwise),
    the last via Proposition~4.23 of O31~v1.5~\cite{Beau2026a35} (single-frequency BI
    fingerprint structure).
    The sub-programme closes the gauge group identification.
    It does not derive Yang--Mills dynamics: that belongs to the gauge--gravity spectral
    stratification sub-programme.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    The gauge structure sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The non-injective projection $\Pi: \chi \to O$ selects admissible configurations from the
    relational substrate.
    Its fibre $\Pi^{-1}(O_n)$ carries internal structure.
    \textit{Which internal symmetry groups are preserved as invariants of admissible projection,
      and why are they precisely $\UU(1)$, $\SU(2)$, and $\SU(3)$?}

    \medskip
    The question is stated precisely as follows.
    Define $\Gpi$ as the group of fibre symmetries of $\Pi$ that preserve the admissibility
    constraints.
    The sub-programme establishes that $\Gpi = \SU(3) \times \SU(2) \times \UU(1)$.

    \medskip
    The physical importance of this identification is that it provides the gauge group of the
    Standard Model as a \emph{forced output} of the admissibility structure, not as a postulate.
    In the Cosmochrony framework, gauge invariance is not introduced as a separate symmetry
    principle: it is the residual fibre symmetry of the projection.
    The gauge bosons and their interactions are subsequently derived as consequences of this
    structure via the spectral entropy functional (Q12, Q13).

    \medskip
    This note is concerned exclusively with the \emph{identification} of the gauge group
    $\Gpi$.
    The derivation of Yang--Mills equations from the vertical variation of the spectral
    entropy belongs to the gauge--gravity spectral stratification sub-programme
    (Presentation Note~8) and is not claimed here.

  \section{Logical Chain of the Sub-Programme}
    \label{sec:chain}

    The derivation chain has four stages.

    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \underbrace{\Pi \simeq S^3}_{\text{fibre topology}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \to S^2}_{\text{Hopf fibration}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\UU(1)}_{\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\su(2) \text{ from } \ImH}_{\text{O23, O27}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\SU(2)}_{\text{O27--O30, Q6a}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\SU(3)}_{\text{O31--O32}}.
    \end{equation}

    The three factors arise from distinct structural mechanisms.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textit{$\UU(1)$ from the Hopf phase fibre.}
      The projection fibre $\Pi \simeq S^3$ carries a canonical Hopf fibration
      $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \to S^2$.
      The circle fibre $S^1$ supports a $\UU(1)$ phase symmetry whose winding number
      $w \in \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the topological origin of electric charge.
      This is a structural result: it follows from the minimal compact geometry required to
      support non-injective stable projections (TopologicalInvariants~\cite{Beau2026k}).

      \item \textit{$\SU(2)$ from quaternionic admissibility.}
      The admissible sector is $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ (spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$).
      The unique associative non-commutative minimal algebra compatible with admissibility
      is $\mathbb{H}$ (O23); $\dim_\mathbb{R} \ImH = 3$ forces three internal directions.
      Any admissible morphism $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ necessarily factors through $\su(2)$
      (O27, quaternionic rigidity theorem).
      The identification chain is:
      \[
        \text{emergence}
        \Rightarrow \su(2)\;[\text{O27}]
        \Rightarrow \text{pair data} \in \Sym(V_\rho, \mathbb{C})\;[\text{O29}]
        \Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3
        \Rightarrow d_\rho = 2\;[\text{O29}].
      \]

      \item \textit{$\SU(3)$ from colour-triplet co-admissibility.}
      The metaplectic intertwining of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ forces triplet
      co-admissibility unconditionally for the colour-adapted Cayley graph (O31).
      $\SU(3)$ is the symmetry group of co-admissible colour triplets
      $\{V_c, V_{\omega c}, V_{\omega^2 c}\}$ with $\omega^3 \equiv 1 \pmod q$,
      $q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$.
      On the standard graph, the identification holds at the rank, averaged,
      effective-exponent, and numerical levels.
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Constituent Papers}

    \subsection{The projective fibre and the \texorpdfstring{$\UU(1)$}{U(1)} phase sector}
      \label{sec:u1}

      The $\UU(1)$ factor arises from the topological structure of the projection fibre.

      \textbf{Foundation~M}~\cite{Beau2026m} establishes that the projection fibre
      $\Pi^{-1}(O_n)$ is identified with the Weil representation $V_\rho$ of
      $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, carrying a canonical symplectic (hence phase) structure.

      \textbf{TopologicalInvariants}~\cite{Beau2026k} classifies the admissible homotopy
      invariants of the projection fibre.
      The projection fibre $\Pi \simeq S^3$ carries the Hopf fibration
      $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\pi_H} S^2$.
      Three results are established.
      (i)~Electric charge arises from $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$: the winding number
      $w \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the topological invariant, giving $q_{\mathrm{eff}} = w \cdot e$.
      This is proved on the canonical admissible model (Proposition~3.1).
      (ii)~On the canonical admissible model, the bounded-flux constraint removes the polar
      regions of the Hopf base $S^2$, reducing the admissible base to an annulus with
      $\pi_2(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{adm}}) = 0$ (Proposition~4.1): no isolated magnetic monopoles
      are topologically admissible.
      (iii)~Parity violation is interpreted as projective chirality: an orientation dependence of
      admissibility under $\Pi$, which selects one handedness of winding invariants.

      \textbf{Q6a}~\cite{Beau2026q6a} derives the gauge group $\Gpi$, gauge charges, and the
      Yang--Mills principal bundle from the fibre structure of $\Pi$.
      The $\UU(1)$ factor is identified as the phase sector of the Weil representation,
      arising from the canonical symplectic structure of $V_\rho$ and its metaplectic lift.
      Q6a constructs the admissible principal bundle $P_{\Gpi}(M, \Gpi)$ with structure group
      $\Gpi$ and connection $A \in \Omega^1(P, \mathfrak{g}_\Pi)$.

      \textbf{SMchargemass}~\cite{Beau2026e} uses the $\UU(1)$ gauge-structure result as a
      downstream input, not as a constituent of its identification.
      Mass, charge, and inertia are interpreted there as saturated-response regimes of the
      same bounded projective mechanism.
      It is therefore an application of the gauge-structure output established here, and is
      listed under Outputs to Downstream Branches rather than as a constituent paper.

    \subsection{Quaternionic admissibility and the \texorpdfstring{$\SU(2)$}{SU(2)} sector}
      \label{sec:su2}

      The $\SU(2)$ factor is treated analytically by the chain O23--O30 and Q6a, conditionally
      on the supplied spinor carrier.

      \textbf{O23}~\cite{Beau2026a27} proves that if the admissible neutral sector is carried
      by an irreducible two-dimensional unitary representation $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ (a
      supplied input), then its traceless sector is $\su(2) \cong \ImH$ with
      $\dim_\mathbb{R} \ImH = 3$ (Theorem~3.1).
      The threshold $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ is a supplied selection rule: the carrier selection
      and the identification of $\Sigma_c$ with that dimension are open bridges.

      \textbf{O27}~\cite{Beau2026a31} proves the quaternionic rigidity theorem: every admissible
      morphism $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ necessarily factors through $\su(2)$.
      This is the analytical cornerstone of the $\SU(2)$ identification.
      It is proved unconditionally (Theorem~4.1 of O27), without reference to the bridge
      conjecture or the finite-$q$ structure.

      \textbf{O28}~\cite{Beau2026a32} establishes asymptotic calibration: the covariance
      $\mathcal{C}_c$ in $\mathrm{End}(H_{\mathrm{eff}})$ has rank exactly 3 for every conjugate
      pair at $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$, with invariant eigenvalue structure
      $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$ (the exact value $\lambda_1/\lambda_2 = 2$ is derived
      analytically in O30).

      \textbf{O29}~\cite{Beau2026a33} identifies the carrier $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \cong \mathrm{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$
      (spin-$1$): $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3$ confirmed for $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$, with $d_\rho = 2$ the
      spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ square-root doublet recovered via the Veronese structure.

      \textbf{O30}~\cite{Beau2026a34} derives analytically the invariant eigenvalue ratio
      $\lambda_1/\lambda_2 = 2$: Born--Infeld parity anti-linearity forces an equatorial
      constraint on the admissible fibre.
      The factor of two is purely structural (squared symmetric-tensor normalisation of the
      central Carnot generator $Z = [X, Y]$).

      \textbf{Q6a}~\cite{Beau2026q6a} closes the $\SU(2)$ identification at the level of the
      admissible principal bundle: the $\SU(2)$ factor is the structure group of the
      non-abelian fibre sector corresponding to the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ module.
      The associated connection is the admissible gauge field.

      \textbf{Q7}~\cite{Beau2026q7} establishes that the three-dimensional internal space
      $H_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Sym^2(V_\rho)$ carries the unique irreducible $\su(2)$-module of
      dimension 3, confirming that the spatial and internal $\su(2)$ structures are
      representation-theoretically locked.

    \subsection{Gauge charges and admissible bundle structure}
      \label{sec:charges}

      \textbf{Q6a}~\cite{Beau2026q6a} constructs the full admissible principal bundle
      $P_{\Gpi}(M, \Gpi)$ with fibre structure matching the three admissibility fixed points of

      Gauge charges are identified as holonomy invariants of the admissible connection $A$.

      \textbf{Q12}~\cite{Beau2026q12} identifies $\Gpi = \SU(3) \times \SU(2) \times \UU(1)$

      The base--fibre decomposition and the horizontal--vertical decoupling lemma

      $a_2$ are independent.

      \textbf{TopologicalInvariants}~\cite{Beau2026k} places the charge identification within
      the topological layer: charge is a $\pi_1$ invariant; the absence of monopoles follows
      from $\pi_2(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{adm}}) = 0$ on the canonical model.

    \subsection{Colour-triplet co-admissibility and the \texorpdfstring{$\SU(3)$}{SU(3)} sector}
      \label{sec:su3}

      The $\SU(3)$ sector arises from the co-admissibility of Weil-block triplets under the
      $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ symmetry of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for
      $q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$.

      \textbf{O31}~\cite{Beau2026a35} develops the full $\SU(3)$ emergence framework.
      Three results are established.
      (i)~Metaplectic intertwining of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ forces triplet
      co-admissibility unconditionally for the colour-adapted Cayley graph
      $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q^{(3)})$ (proved).
      (ii)~$\SU(3)$ is identified as the symmetry group of the co-admissible colour triplet
      $\{V_c, V_{\omega c}, V_{\omega^2 c}\}$, proved for the standard graph via
      $[{\mathrm{H\text{-}color}}]$ (Proposition~4.23 of O31, proved analytically).
      (iii)~The spectral route to $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$ is closed: exact generator-twist
      isospectrality does not imply colour-triplet capacity profile equality (proved, closing
      the spectral approach).

      \textbf{O32}~\cite{Beau2026a36} provides the numerical test and the partial analytical
      resolution of $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$.
      Four levels are established.
      $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{rank}}$: sector rank equality
      $r_c(n) = r_{\omega c}(n) = r_{\omega^2 c}(n)$ proved unconditionally via
      intra-coset Vandermonde symmetry (O31).
      $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{avg}}$: block-averaged capacity equality
      $\mathbb{E}[\sigma_c(n)] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma_{\omega c}(n)] + O(q^{-1})$,
      proved in O32 via the modulation frequency argument.
      $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{eff}}$: effective exponent equality
      $\delta_c = \delta_{\omega c} = \delta_{\omega^2 c}$ in the $q \to \infty$ limit, proved in O32.
      $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$: exact profile equality at finite $q$,
      proved analytically in O31 (Proposition~4.23) via the single-frequency fingerprint
      structure: each O12 fingerprint vector carries frequency
      $B = c_1 b_1 + c_2 b_2 + c_3 b_3$, and the orbit dilation $B \mapsto \omega B$ is a
      bijection of $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$.
      Numerical results: passes for $q \in \{61, 151, 211, 307\}$ ($q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$,
      test primes); controls $q \in \{29, 101\}$ ($q \equiv 2 \pmod 3$) confirm noise floor.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The gauge structure sub-programme identifies the admissible internal symmetry group
    $\Gpi = \SU(3) \times \SU(2) \times \UU(1)$ as a forced output of the admissibility
    constraints on the projection fibre $\Pi$.

    The three factors have distinct origins and distinct analytical status.
    $\UU(1)$ arises from the Hopf phase fibre of $\Pi \simeq S^3$; charge quantisation
    follows from $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
    $\SU(2)$ arises from the supplied spinor carrier, whose neutral sector is
    $\su(2) \cong \ImH$ (O23 Theorem~3.1), and the rigidity theorem of O27; its closure is
    conditional on that carrier.
    $\SU(3)$ arises from the metaplectic co-admissibility of Weil-block colour triplets;
    it is established unconditionally on the colour-adapted graph and at all four analytical
    levels ($[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{rank}}$, $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{avg}}$,
    $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$)
    on the standard graph, the last via Proposition~4.23 of O31~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
    The sub-programme closes the gauge group identification without residual conditionality.

    This sub-programme closes the gauge group identification.
    It does not derive Yang--Mills dynamics: the $a_4$ Yang--Mills response from the
    vertical variation of the spectral entropy belongs to Note~8
    (Gauge--Gravity Stratification).
